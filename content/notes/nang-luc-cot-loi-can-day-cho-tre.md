这篇文章讨论的是：孩子在高中毕业之前，除了考试成绩，还应该形成哪些能够支撑大学学习、职业发展和长期人生选择的通用能力。吴军认为，最关键的是四种能力：逻辑思维、写作与表达、实验能力、规划能力。它们并非临近毕业才突击训练，而应贯穿整个中小学阶段，并且越早开始越好。

第一，逻辑思维能力。  
作者认为，当前教育中相对欠缺的并不是数理化知识，而是系统的逻辑训练。逻辑是科学思维的基础，包含从已有知识出发进行演绎推理，以及从经验和实验中归纳规律。拥有逻辑能力的人，面对从未做过的复杂任务时，不会只依赖模仿或直觉，而能把问题拆开、逐步推导出可行路径。它还能帮助人识别他人观点中的漏洞，提高判断力，避免轻信；同时让思考和表达更清晰，并能从个别经验中提炼可复用的方法，而不是以偏概全。

第二，写作与表达能力。  
写作不是单纯为了写作业，而是为了准确组织思想、与他人协作和影响他人。口头表达则是把思想清晰地传递出去的能力。作者指出，AI 可以帮助纠正语法、润色措辞，却无法替代一个人形成真正有内容、有结构的想法。孩子需要在家庭分享、演讲、辩论、读书会和社交活动中练习：听懂别人真正的意思，也让别人理解并接受自己的观点。语言能力的边界，也会影响一个人理解世界的边界。

第三，实验能力。  
文章强调，实验精神不只属于物理、化学和生物课，更是一种低成本获取真实反馈、降低风险的生活方法。比如尝试一种陌生食物，先买少量；学习做菜，通过控制变量调整调料比例；尝试投资，先用小金额了解自己对亏损的心理承受能力，而不是一开始就重仓甚至加杠杆。实验的关键，是以小步尝试代替盲目押注，在反馈中修正判断。对于人生选择、职业探索、投资甚至人际关系，也都可以借鉴这种思路。

第四，规划能力。  
规划不同于梦想、目标或战略，它是把目标转化为行动的工具。作者提出规划有两个核心：一是计算资源，弄清自己拥有什么、能投入什么；二是发现矛盾，排除不可能的方案。规划必须从现实出发，而不是从“我理想中将会达到的状态”出发。例如，高三学生选择学校和专业，应先基于当前成绩制定方案，再根据后续进步调整，而不是预设自己必然提高很多分。最后，规划的价值取决于执行。没有执行、总给自己找例外的规划，实际上没有意义。

文章最后的核心结论是：教育不应只培养会考试的人，而应帮助孩子建立一套处理未知问题的底层能力。逻辑帮助判断，表达帮助协作与影响，实验帮助学习和降低风险，规划帮助把有限资源投入到长期目标中。

## English Summary

This article discusses the abilities children should develop before graduating from high school, beyond academic performance. Wu Jun argues that four core skills are essential for university, career development, and life: logical thinking, writing and communication, experimentation, and planning. These abilities should not be crammed shortly before graduation, but cultivated throughout primary and secondary education.

1. Logical thinking  
The author believes that the major gap in education is not mathematics or science, but systematic training in logic. Logic is the foundation of scientific thinking: using deduction to reason from existing knowledge and using induction to draw lessons from experience and experiments. It helps people break down unfamiliar problems, make sound judgments, detect flaws in others’ arguments, communicate clearly, and turn individual experiences into reusable methods.

2. Writing and communication  
Writing is not merely for school assignments. It helps people organize their thoughts, collaborate with others, and influence decisions. Speaking is the ability to communicate those thoughts clearly. AI can help improve grammar and wording, but it cannot replace a person’s own thinking. Children need opportunities to practice through family sharing, speeches, debates, reading groups, and social activities. They need to learn both to understand what others really mean and to express their own ideas convincingly.

3. Experimentation  
Experimentation is not limited to science classes. It is a practical method for gaining feedback and reducing risk in everyday life. When trying unfamiliar food, learn from a small sample rather than buying too much. When cooking, adjust one variable at a time. When considering investing, start with a small amount to understand your real tolerance for losses instead of making a large, emotional bet. The core idea is to make small trials, learn from feedback, and revise decisions rather than committing blindly from the beginning.

4. Planning  
Planning is different from dreams, goals, strategy, or tactics. It is the practical tool that turns goals into action. Good planning involves two things: calculating available resources and identifying contradictions or unrealistic assumptions. Plans should start from present reality, not an ideal future version of oneself. For example, students should choose university options based on their current academic level, then adjust as results improve. Most importantly, a plan only has value if it is executed consistently.

The article concludes that education should not merely produce students who perform well on exams. It should help young people build a durable toolkit for dealing with uncertainty: logic for judgment, communication for cooperation and influence, experimentation for learning and risk reduction, and planning for allocating limited resources toward long-term goals.
